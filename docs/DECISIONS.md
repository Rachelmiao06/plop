# Decisions

One line per decision: date, what, why.

- 2026-11-24 · Name is Plop. Short, cute, sounds like a snowball landing.
- 2026-11-24 · Slingshot aiming: pull back, shoot forward. Easy to read, allows a preview arc.
- 2026-11-24 · Ball size = the farthest pull reached; easing back does not shrink it. Lets you adjust aim without losing the charge.
- 2026-11-24 · Only the snowball flies; the character returns to a fixed spot. Simpler, keeps the two characters the same distance apart.
- 2026-11-24 · Camera above and to the side, characters large. A straight top-down view made them too small.
- 2026-11-24 · No scores, no online play in the first version. It is a toy; two people share one device.
- 2026-11-24 · Realistic snow ground, AI-drawn half-3D layered characters. Art that an image model can make.
- 2026-11-24 · Feel prototype with plain shapes comes before any art. The project succeeds or fails on feel.
- 2026-11-24 · Scene units: one unit is one character height. The camera keeps a character at a fifth of the screen height on every screen, so narrow screens show less width instead of smaller characters.
- 2026-11-24 · On screens too narrow for two characters side by side (phone portrait), the two spots are staggered front-to-back, left one further away, and stay the same distance apart. Keeps the throw distance equal on every device. Other features read the spots from `useSnowLayout()`.
- 2026-11-24 · The snow fades into a fog colour in its own shader (not Three.js scene fog), and the backdrop is that same colour. Scene fog is applied before tone mapping and left a visible seam at the mesh edge.
- 2026-11-24 · The camera sways very slightly all the time. Sparkles only glint when the view shifts, and the view is otherwise fixed.
- 2026-11-24 · Other features use the snow only through `useSnow()` and `useSnowLayout()` from `@/features/snow-scene`, as children of `<SnowScene>`. Pointer rays skip the snow mesh (too many triangles); aim at a flat plane and correct with `heightAt`.
- 2026-11-24 · Pressing adds up: one touch is shallow, holding digs down to hard ground, and the snow layer is 0.9 units thick (was 0.5) so shallow and deep read differently from the camera. Rachel: the first version lacked depth.
- 2026-11-24 · Digging depth and friction are decided by the caller, not the snow: `pressGroove` takes `dig` and a velocity, and `packedAt` reports resistance. The snowball feature can then make a big ball feel heavier with its own numbers.
- 2026-11-24 · Dust and clumps are thrown by the snow itself whenever it is pressed or hit, so every feature gets them without extra code.
- 2026-11-24 · Zoom is written by hand, not drei CameraControls: the camera only moves along its fixed viewing line, and the view is clamped back to the centre at full size. Two unclaimed fingers are a pinch; a feature calls `claimSnowPointer` to keep a finger for its own drag.
- 2026-11-24 · The snow stays a CPU height grid (not a GPU displacement map), because later features need `heightAt`. Measured cost is small.
- 2026-11-24 · Snow rolling and landing sounds are real CC0 recordings from Freesound (`public/assets/audio/`, credits there), sliced and re-pitched by the engine; only the whoosh is synthesized. Pure synthesis sounded like scratching paper, not fluffy snow. This replaces issue #9's "no sound files".
- 2026-11-24 · Snow fills the screen at every angle and zoom; the slab with a cut side was tried and rejected because the empty surround looked wrong (Rachel). The play area is a fine pressable grid, surrounded by one big cheap snow surface that shares its height function, material and edge points so no seam shows; far away it melts into a soft haze.
- 2026-11-24 · Camera rules: starts above and to the side (35 degrees). One finger or mouse drag on empty snow turns it, 25 degrees up to 82 (nearly top-down) and 60 degrees left and right, never behind or below, with a little glide after a flick. It does not spring back; a "Reset view" button does that. Two fingers / wheel zoom. The view pulls back by itself when turned so both characters stay in frame.
- 2026-11-24 · A feature keeps a finger for its own drag with `claimSnowPointer`; claimed fingers never turn or zoom the view. This is how the slingshot drag will coexist with look-around.
- 2026-11-24 · Because the view turns, the two characters will be small 3D snowmen, not flat stickers.
- 2026-11-24 · The look is "crayon sticker snow village": hand-drawn stickers with warm brown pencil outlines, coloured-pencil fills, round shapes and handwritten lettering. Picked by Rachel from a reference illustration.
- 2026-11-24 · Outlines and text are warm brown (`--ink`), never black. Black looks harsh next to crayon colours.
- 2026-11-24 · Shadows are pale blue (`--snow-shadow`), never grey. The 3D snow's shadow tint should match `--snow-shadow` so the drawn stickers and the real snow belong together.
- 2026-11-24 · Colours, fonts and radius live as tokens in `src/app/globals.css`; `/dev/tokens` shows them all. The palette values are fixed; when text on a colour was too faint, the text colour changed, not the palette. Two helper colours were added for that: `--ink-deep` (text on sky-blue and mint) and `--berry-deep` (warning text; plain berry is too faint as text on paper).
- 2026-11-24 · Headings use Gaegu (handwritten), small text uses Nunito (round, easy to read). Corners are generous and round (`--radius` 1.25rem).
- 2026-11-24 · Light theme only. The dark variant resolves to the same colours so the page never turns black.
- 2026-11-24 · Style notes for future art: outline about 1.5% of the character's height, with a slight hand wobble and round caps; coloured-pencil hatch fills, no gradients; characters about two heads tall, dot eyes, a short arc mouth, pink blush.
- 2026-11-24 · Thickness is shown by the snow itself, not a cut side: dents and pits with shaded walls and rims, depth that builds with dwell time, drifts that catch the low sun, and bigger mounds in the distance.
- 2026-11-24 · Fine ripples are shading only everywhere; small drifts are real shape near the middle and shading only further out, because the far grid is too coarse to carry them. The hand-over distances are in `content.ts` under `surround`.
- 2026-11-24 · Camera limits unchanged after the slab was removed (25 to 82 degrees up, 60 left and right): at the lowest angle the camera still looks at least 14 degrees below the horizon, and the snow has turned to haze well before its end.
- 2026-11-24 · Pointers aim at the snow as it was before pressing, not at the dented surface. Aiming at dents made the target jump at every pit and rim and made a held press creep away as its own pit deepened; this was the main reason dragging did not feel smooth.
- 2026-11-24 · Drag resistance is light and capped: the pressed point trails the finger by about a tenth of a unit at normal speed and never more than 0.35. The first version trailed by almost a full unit, which read as stutter (Rachel, on a Mac).
- 2026-11-24 · The picture is drawn with at most 3.2 million pixels (slightly below full retina on a big window). Script work is negligible; nearly all frame time is filling pixels, so this is the safety margin for slower computers. Phones are unaffected.
- 2026-11-24 · Zooming out flies up to a high view: 5.5 times the normal distance, tipping to 76 degrees, in one eased glide. Not straight down, because later scenery will be upright cut-outs that face the camera. Snow reaches 92 units out and haze starts at 44, so the high view is all snow.
- 2026-11-24 · The camera moves along one path instead of separate zoom and tilt: low and close looking across the snow (14 degrees) through the play view (35) to high, far and steep (76, 5.5 times the distance). Scroll, pinch and up/down drag move along it; sideways swipe and drag turn. Rachel: scrolling should change the viewpoint, not just the distance.
- 2026-11-24 · Scroll direction follows Mac natural scrolling like a map: fingers up (and pinch together) = rise and pull back. `cameraInput.invertScroll` swaps it.
- 2026-11-24 · A trackpad swipe locks to its main axis after 6 pixels, so a mostly vertical swipe never spins the view. Mouse wheel notches are a fixed step (6% of the path) regardless of browser; trackpad deltas are capped at 60 px per event, so fast flicks glide instead of jumping. Input slows over the last 12% of the path and the turn, so the view eases into its limits.
- 2026-11-24 · Camera control is never blocked while characters idle; a feature takes a pointer away from the camera with `claimSnowPointer`.
- 2026-11-24 · Snow can be pressed anywhere (up to 60 units from the middle), not inside a fixed area: Rachel did not want pressing limited to a square. Pressed spots are stored in 4-unit tiles made on demand (0.05 squares everywhere so thin far tracks stay smooth, at most 400 tiles); the plain snow hides itself under each tile. The fine ripples became shading only, so tiles and plain snow match exactly.
- 2026-11-24 · The test press keeps its real size on the snow at any distance: close up a drag leaves a wide groove, from far a thin one (Rachel). Growing it with distance was tried and rejected because from far it looked too coarse.
- 2026-11-24 · Kept the custom path camera instead of switching to drei CameraControls: a request to switch arrived, but Rachel then said the path camera feels good.
- 2026-11-24 · Look comes first: hand-drawn feel, cuteness and matching the design tokens are the acceptance bar for every visual change, checked against Rachel's reference art. Recorded in CLAUDE.md.
- 2026-11-24 · Game is paper bowling on snow (docs/GAMEPLAY.md draft 2): a paper thrower at a line pulls back to grow a snowball, which rolls through a crowd of paper cut-outs that topple. Chosen over snowmen running around a field (draft 1) because hand-drawn feel matters most and every piece can be Rachel's paper art; it also keeps the camera in front of the cut-outs. Fixed spots facing each other felt strange.
- 2026-11-24 · Defaults Claude picked for draft 2, open to change: the ball rolls on the ground (not an arc), two players take turns, the thrower is a paper standee too, the 3D snowmen are parked.
- 2026-11-24 · The game keeps a score (one point per cut-out down, small full-clear bonus), shown in a floating, frosted, hand-drawn panel with a progress bar for the current set. Rachel: hitting alone is not enough. Final design by Rachel later.
- 2026-11-24 · Fresh snowfall (button, N key, `freshSnowfall()`) makes the snow fall harder, then fills dents from the bottom and fades rims and tint over about 3 seconds before freeing the tiles; never a cut or a reset, so it reads as new snow settling.
- 2026-11-24 · The score display (`src/features/hud`) only shows the numbers and events it is given; no game rules inside. Music has its own volume and on / off switch, separate from a sound-effects switch, both remembered on the device and placed in the HUD corner.
- 2026-11-24 · Scenery is flat hand-drawn cut-outs standing upright in the snow that turn to face the camera; only the two main characters are 3D. Keeps the sticker look and lets the image model make every prop (`src/features/scenery/`).
- 2026-11-24 · Standee pillow shading is off by default and the card edge is uneven like real card: next to Rachel's sheets the faint normal-map tint read as computer-made. Scenery is laid out as lane framing for paper bowling; the thrower cut-outs are the front views of the two turnaround sheets.
- 2026-11-24 · Bowling throw: the aim line and the real roll come from one function with the same fixed step (`planRoll` / `stepRoll`), and aim comes from where the ball is (it lags the finger), not the finger. The ball stops exactly where the line ends; changing either breaks that. A rolling ball never passes `dig` to `pressGroove` (it stacks to hard ground); only a ball sitting still digs.
- 2026-11-25 · Pins topple with Rapier (`@react-three/rapier` 2.2.0, `src/features/pins/`): cards face the throwing line and never half-turn while physics runs, the ball is a kinematic sphere that keeps the bowling path, and fallen cards are all held still and hop back together so a reset never knocks a standing card.
- 2026-11-24 · The village stands on the real snow fitted to the bowling lane (layout stretched in depth, sides moved out by the extra slide room); bases sit in heaps of real snow from the snow scene's new `heapSnow` (kept through fresh snowfall), not a paper mound. The game starts in a throwing view set from outside with `useSnowCameraView` (look-at point, path position, turn, distance); Reset view returns to it. While the village shows, sun shadows are lightened to 0.55 so they read pale blue next to paper.
- 2026-11-25 · The snow is painted in tokens, not rendered: `--paper` where lit, one flat `--snow-shadow` wash for cast shadows and shaded slopes (`content.shade`, exported as `snowShadow`; the single place shadow strength is set, the village override is gone), pressed snow pale blue to `--sky-blue` with depth. The snow skips tone mapping (Neutral turns paper white into light grey); lit 3D things like the bowling card and ball keep it. Replaces the lilac, lit-render look and the 0.55 village shadow setting.
- 2026-11-25 · Pins are airy: 5 to 8 small friends per formation, no card hiding more than 15% of another from the throwing view (checked on `/dev/pins` by `src/features/pins/overlap.ts`), and a calm patch of open snow at least 3 units deep behind them, with the big tree moved back right. The throwers (bucket-hat and red-hat snowmen) are never pins; the sliding penguin and the skier look like they are moving, so they are village decoration at the lane's edges with a small loop. Rachel: the old sets were one crowded clump. Fewer, airier pins topple a little less per throw; that trade is deliberate.
- 2026-11-25 · The thrower is the scenery thick standee of Rachel's snowmen (bucket = player one, red hat = two) with hand-drawn springy motion, and the snowball is drawn, not lit: `--paper` base, hatched `--snow-shadow` side fixed to a drawing light turned to the side so the shade shows from the throwing view, pencil marks that turn with the roll, an uneven `--ink` outline shell. No PBR material on either (#37).
- 2026-11-25 · The home page is the game (`src/features/game/`): one ball per turn, turns alternate Bucket → Red Hat, one point per card down plus 3 for a full clear, the turn passes 0.25 s after the pins stand again, and no pull can start while pins settle. A ball left resting melts 1.1 s after the count so the reset never waits for it. Feel numbers: `docs/TUNING.md`.
- 2026-11-25 · The throwing view never sees the horizon (its top edge looks 13° down), so sky and far hills are a curved paper backdrop standing just behind the village like the back of a paper theatre (`backdrop` in scenery `content.ts`), opaque so the snow behind it is not drawn, fading out above 38° so the high view stays all snow. Changing the throwing view to show a real horizon was rejected: it would flatten the lane the game is framed for (#38).
- 2026-11-25 · The snow is real, lit snow again (the flat paper-tone pass of #36 is undone; Rachel: "make the snow's real feel like it was before"), while the cut-outs stay exactly as drawn (unlit paper shaders, token colours, no tone mapping). Shade comes from a clear pale sky-blue fill (`#a9cdf5`) instead of the old blue-violet, which kept the depth without the lilac cast; cast shadows take 0.8 of the sun (`content.shade.strength`, the sun's shadow intensity), never a Village override. A `look.realism` slider was considered and dropped: the restored look is the default and one more knob was not worth the complexity (#38).
- 2026-11-25 · Vibration is one shared engine (`src/lib/haptics.ts`) with named patterns, rate limits and a 260 ms-per-second cap, and it follows the sound-effects switch rather than a switch of its own (one "feedback" switch is simpler; silencing the toy should also stop it buzzing). Hit-stop is owned by the bowling game (`hitStop` / `timeScale`); the pins pause their physics and step it by hand while it runs. No camera nudge: the snow scene exposes none (#10).
- 2026-11-25 · The throw is a snap, not a shot (Rachel: too slow, no punch): the ball leaves at 35% speed and reaches full in 0.09 s, the snow barely slows it for the first 0.6 s, and a light ball reaches the pins in about 0.7 s, a full one in about 1.25 s; the aim line still uses the same `stepRoll`. The snowman rolls on its round bottom instead of gliding. The camera follow is a small offset added in `src/features/game/CameraFollow.tsx` after the snow scene's rig places the camera, because `useSnowCameraView` only sets the home view and jumps there; replace it with a snow-scene look offset when one exists.
- 2026-11-25 · The throw is filmed (Rachel: the camera felt static, the lane short): the snow scene's camera takes shots (`setSnowCameraShot`, `kickSnowCamera`) as an offset on top of the player's own view, pulled by critically damped springs so it never jumps and never lags the player's own turning; the game directs aim → launch → chase → impact → return (the camera never goes past the snowman, Rachel: it follows the ball by zooming, not by flying down the lane), and any drag, scroll or key hands the view back. This replaces `CameraFollow.tsx`. The lane is 8.6 long (was 5.6); the village stretches only between the line and the pins, so the far cluster and the paper sky stay as close behind the pins as before.
- 2026-11-25 · The picture gets its finish from plain CSS above the canvas (`src/features/look/`: a faint `--ink` paper grain and a soft fade into `--paper` at the bottom and corners), not from post-processing: an EffectComposer renders into a target, where three.js skips tone mapping, so either the lit snow or the unlit paper would change. Tilt-shift, bloom, Sparkles, Clouds, ContactShadows, SoftShadows and Motion were reviewed and not added (docs/REVIEW.md, #42). Wired into the home page only after the throw-camera PR, which owns `src/features/game/`, merges.
- 2026-11-25 · A phone held upright (width / height under 0.8) gets its own throwing view from the game (`GAME.phoneView`: lower on the camera path, further back, looking a little further down the lane), so the pins sit near the middle with the village below the score panel and no empty snow under the thrower; wider screens keep the scenery's `throwingView`. Two framings, never a blend, so resizing never makes the camera jump. On phones the corner toggles are one row and the panel takes the full width below; the "+1" lands on the bar's fill end, not the label. The paper finish (`<PaperFinish />`) is on the home page (#46, #50).
- 2026-11-25 · The village is a little street from Rachel's doodle-8/9/10 sheets: only eight of the twelve new drawings are placed (Rachel: no need to use them all, just a slightly bigger, richer scene), the homes stand forward of a back row (Christmas tree, café, cocoa stall, red cottage) so no roof hides it, the Christmas tree stays beside the pins, never behind them (it hid the green walking-pine pin in the impact shot), and no crayon prop stands in the village (the doodle lamp took the name `street-lamp`). Props carry optional effect tags (`emitsLight`, `chimneyAt`, `icicleEdge`). A phone held upright gets its own few small cut-outs low in the bottom corners (`view: "portrait"`), since it sees only the lane. The far hills are Rachel's tileable panorama, copies side by side instead of mirrored (no symmetric bump).
- 2026-11-25 · The hit reads at once (#47, #48, #52): a ball that has rolled through the pins bursts into snow (`burst()` in bowling, called by the pins) because from the low impact camera even a ball resting behind the set covers the fallen cards; the pins' `result` now fires as soon as the count is certain instead of after the ball stops (the game needs no change). Fallen cards are propped 16° off the snow on a paper-snow heap in the drawing only (30° read as still standing; a real `heapSnow` heap is permanent, so not used); no "x x" face, as the art has none. The pulled ball grows beside the thrower's feet, so its roll starts beside the snowman rather than behind it.
- 2026-11-25 · Village ambience (`startAmbience`, wind with gusts, the cocoa-corner fire, distant chimes or sleigh bells, a chickadee) and the named cues (`playRoundStart`, `playStarHit`, `playNearMiss`, `playFullClear`, `playWin`, `playTie`, `playTap`) run on the effects bus, so the sound-effects switch silences them and the music switch does not; the ambience has its own volume and no switch of its own (one less button). Recordings are CC0 and loaded only when first needed; bells, sparkle and jingles are synthesized so they stay soft and tunable in `AMBIENCE` / `CUES`.
- 2026-11-25 · Real light, smoke and ice on the paper village (Rachel): warm glow sprites plus point lights that light only the real snow, procedural smoke puffs tinted toward `--snow-shadow` instead of the drawn smoke doodle, and icicles and ice chunks with a faked transmission shader (no screen-space transmission, for phones); the sun moved up to 42° at strength 3.1 so shadows are half as long and paler (#45) while the snow stays lit; a soft bank of real snow at the backdrop's foot hides the low view's line (#51).
- 2026-11-25 · App shell: a paper loading screen (`src/features/loading/`) sits over the game in `src/app/page.tsx` and decides "ready" by itself (canvas has drawn, drei `useProgress` idle, at least 0.9 s, at most 15 s), so the game needs no change; an optional `plop:ready` window event from the game would end it sooner. Icons are crops of the bucket-hat thrower (head for tab sizes, whole snowman for the Apple icon) and the share card is a static 1200×630 composite of her cut-outs and the Gaegu title (`src/app/opengraph-image.png`), not a generated `ImageResponse` route, so it is exactly her art with no font fetch at build. CI runs `scripts/smoke.mjs` as its own job: every page, the icons and the share card must answer, and the server must log no error. Sharing (`src/features/share/`): WeChat ignores Open Graph, so the home page starts with an invisible 600×600 copy of the card art as its first body image, and the share button shows a "tap ···" note inside WeChat instead of calling an API it blocks; a rich WeChat card would need our own domain, ICP filing, a verified Official Account and a signing server, all deliberately not done.
- 2026-11-25 · A match moves from afternoon to sunset to a cosy evening through one value, `setTimeOfDay(t)` owned by the snow scene (0 = afternoon, 1 = evening), which the snow's light, the village's lights and smoke, and the paper sky all follow. Sunset stays bright and peach-lilac (a too-low, weak sun made the snow murky brown), evening is a pale moonlit blue with more exposure; light colours are mixed from token values and the cut-outs are never lit.
- 2026-11-25 · Time of day is one shared value, t from 0 (afternoon) through 0.5 (sunset) to 1 (evening), read with `getTimeOfDay()`; the sky follows it in flat token-colour bands with grainy edges (never a smooth gradient), with Rachel's sun setting behind the hills, her crescent moon rising and the star doodle as tiny stars. No new art and no new colours: every sky colour is a mix of two tokens.
- 2026-11-25 · A match replaces endless turns (Rachel changed her mind on draft 2's "no rounds, no winner screen"): choose 3 or 5 rounds (or "just me" against your own best), each player throws once a round at a new set that asks a different shot (off-centre, split, slanted behind a soft mound, the rabbit to leave standing, a star friend for +2), a sweet spot on the pull (past it the ball is heavy and the aim sways, shown by pencil ticks, no meter), and a kind end card; knocking the rabbit scores nothing rather than taking points away. Measured with a headless replay of the real roll and physics (`src/features/game/measure`, `docs/TUNING.md`): full power straight at the middle is no longer the best shot in any round set.
- 2026-11-25 · A match grows from afternoon (round 1) to cosy evening (`GAME.evening`: the last round at 0.85, the end card at 1, for 3 and 5 rounds alike); the light only glides after a round's last throw, during "N down!" and the set change, never while a ball rolls, and "play again" fades back to afternoon. The match plays the named cues from `src/lib/audio.ts` (round chime, star sparkle, bunny mew on a near miss that leaves it standing, full-clear bells instead of the pins' little chime, win or tie, paper taps on the start and end cards) and the ambience bed after the first tap, a little lower while the music plays.
- 2026-11-25 · Score panel, light polish for #31 (not a redesign; Rachel may still redo it): paper first, glass second. Panels are 70% paper white (was 52; up to 86 by evening, from the snow scene's time of day) with `--ink` grain and a short hatched coloured-pencil wash, `--snow-shadow` in the afternoon turning `--butter` by evening so the end card reads as a lamp-lit paper note on the dark snow. Pencil outlines vary in weight along the stroke, the bar hatch is uneven, the active player has a `--butter` crayon ring instead of a gradient glow, and faces, star and surprise lines are Rachel's art (thrower crops, `public/assets/effects/`); the crown stays a stand-in.
- 2026-11-25 · "Together" mode: both snowmen on the line at once (two bowling games, each on its half of the line, page-wide ball ids), Bucket on A / D + W (or Space), Red Hat on ← / → + ↑ (or Enter), chosen as two far-apart key groups so a Mac keyboard does not ghost; each throws once a round and the count waits for both, each card counts for the ball that reached it first (chains included), no filmed chase. The mode choice is a paper tab the game places on top of the hud's start card until the hud adds a `modes` row (the hud is another session's).
- 2026-11-25 · Rachel: keep the classic bowling-pin crowd every round; only small shifts. Each round set is a classic formation (triangle, crowd of friends, ring, a ten-pin triangle, the village) standing in the middle of the lane, changed only by a sideways shift of at most 0.3, a turn of a few degrees, who stands where, and one star friend or the bunny inside or just in front; small soft mounds only from round 4 and off to the sides. The off-to-the-side and split sets are gone; a straight throw at the middle may score well.
- 2026-11-25 · Village bystanders are never alone in open snow (Rachel): each small character stands beside the thing it belongs with, as a little moment (the cocoa mug character and the otter queuing at the tree-stump café, the mouse with its letter queuing behind the penguins at the postbox, the red sled resting by the blue cottage's door). The portrait-only cut-outs in the foreground corners are gone; a phone held upright simply sees less of the sides, with no foreground filler. Each round of a match adds one small rule to the classic crowd (Strike!, Hit the star, Let the bunny be, Little mound, Sleepy cat).
- 2026-11-25 · Friends invite each other with a link, `/invite?g=<group>&from=<name>&score=<n>` (name capped at 12 characters, nothing else personal; `g` is 8 random URL-safe characters made on a person's first invite and remembered on the device, kept when an invite is re-shared, so one group shares one leaderboard): it opens a Christmas-evening card over the real scene, and "接受挑战" goes to `/?g=<group>&from=<name>`. Inside WeChat the sender is taken to the card page and shown the "···" note, because only WeChat's own menu makes a proper chat card without an Official Account. The loading bar is a tiny raw-WebGL strip of real snow (no three.js), with the pencil line as the no-WebGL fallback.
- 2026-11-25 · Group leaderboards, no login (Rachel): this changes "no database, no accounts" to **a small database, still no accounts**. Each invite group (`g`, 8 URL-safe characters) has a board; whoever plays from its link types a nickname (remembered on the device only) and their match score goes on it. Stored per group and nickname (case-insensitive): best score with its rounds and mode and when, latest score and rounds, matches played; nothing else (no IP, email or device id). Storage switches in `src/lib/db.ts`: Supabase when `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` exist on the server (table `scores`, `supabase/migrations/0001_scores.sql`, row-level security on with no public policies, one atomic `submit_score` upsert), otherwise demo mode in memory and a temp file. Every read and write goes through `/api/scores` with the server key; the server refuses bad group ids, nicknames outside 1 to 12 characters or on a small English / Chinese block-list, rounds other than 3 or 5, modes other than turns / together, and scores above what a match can make (`maxScore`, from the game and pins tuning), and allows 10 submits a minute per IP and group. 3- and 5-round scores share one board.
- 2026-11-25 · Visit analytics are Vercel Web Analytics + Speed Insights only (cookieless, URLs sent without their query except `utm_*`, so invite names, groups and scores never leave the page); custom events go through `track()` in `src/lib/analytics.ts`, which always adds `source` (wechat / home_screen / browser) and, because Vercel Pro keeps only 2 properties per event, folds two or more game properties into one `detail` string.
